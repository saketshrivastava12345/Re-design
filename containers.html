Reading the table

Why the numbers on your booking may differ

These are nominal figures for the standard ISO types. Real boxes vary by manufacturer, age and owner, and two 40′ high cubes from different lessors will not have identical tares.

  • Tare and payload are printed on the door. The maximum payload is the box's rated gross weight minus its actual tare — always check the plate.
  • Road limits usually bite first. A container rated for 28 tonnes may still be restricted to far less by the axle-load rules on the haulage leg.
  • Cubic capacity is not usable capacity. Pallet footprint, stacking height and dunnage typically cost you 10–15% of the nominal cube.
  • VGM is mandatory. Under SOLAS the verified gross mass must be declared before loading; we file it with the booking.

Choosing a box

Which container for which cargo

Dry

General purpose

Almost everything. Choose a high cube when the cargo is light and bulky — the extra 30 cm of height is usually cheaper than a second box.

Reefer

Temperature controlled

Pharma, food and chemicals with a temperature range. Note the smaller internal cube: the machinery takes roughly half a metre of length.

Open top

Over-height cargo

When the cargo has to be craned in from above or stands taller than the door. Tarpaulin and bows in place of a fixed roof.

Flat rack

Out-of-gauge

Machinery, vehicles and anything wider than a box. Priced on out-of-gauge dimensions rather than on cube.

Ventilated

Breathing cargo

Coffee, cocoa and other cargo that sweats. Passive vents, not refrigeration.

Tank

Liquid and gas in bulk

T11 tanks for chemicals and gases, with dedicated empty yards at our import gateways. ISO Tank
