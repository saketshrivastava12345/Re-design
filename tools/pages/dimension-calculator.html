The calculation

What you will actually be charged on

Freight is charged on the greater of the real weight and the space the cargo occupies. This works out both, for sea and for air, using the same rules our quotes are built on.

Dimension Calculator

Dimensions are per piece and the total is multiplied by the number of pieces. Gross weight is the total for the consignment.

The rules behind it

Two divisors, one principle

Sea

1 CBM = 1,000 kg

LCL ocean freight is charged on the revenue tonne: whichever is greater, the volume in cubic metres or the weight in metric tonnes.

Volume = L × W × H (cm) ÷ 1,000,000 × pieces
Volumetric weight = CBM × 1,000 kg

Dense cargo is charged on weight; light, bulky cargo on volume.

Air

IATA divisor 6,000

Air freight uses the IATA volumetric divisor of 6,000 cm³ per kilogram.

Volumetric weight = L × W × H (cm) ÷ 6,000 × pieces

A 120 × 100 × 100 cm piece is 200 kg volumetric no matter what it weighs on the scale.

This is an estimate, not a quotation. Carriers round up, some lanes apply a different divisor by contract, and surcharges are outside this calculation. Use it to size the shipment and to sanity-check a rate — then ask for the real number.

Getting the number down

Four things that change the answer

  1. Measure the outside, not the goods

    Carriers measure the outermost point of the packed piece, pallet feet and shrink wrap included. A pallet overhang of two centimetres on each side is chargeable volume.

  2. Check whether stacking is allowed

    A pallet marked do-not-stack is charged on the full height to the roof of the container or ULD, not on its own height. If your cargo can take a top load, say so.

  3. Compare against a full container

    Once your CBM approaches half a box, run the FCL numbers. Our container table has the usable cube for every standard type.

  4. Split by density, not by order

    On a mixed consignment, the heavy items decide the sea charge and the bulky items decide the air charge. Splitting them across modes sometimes beats sending everything one way.
