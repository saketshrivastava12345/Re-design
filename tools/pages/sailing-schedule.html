Why we do not publish a timetable

A printed schedule is out of date before it loads

Sailing schedules move constantly. Vessels are omitted, rotations are changed, blank sailings are declared at a week's notice and transhipment connections slip. A published table would be wrong often enough to be worse than nothing — and the one time it mattered, it would be wrong then too.

What the desk sends you instead is the current position on your specific lane: which vessels are open, what the CFS cut-off is, what the documentation cut-off is, and where the box transships. That is the information a booking is actually made on.

1650 destinations are serviced by LCL weekly, so for most lanes the answer is a choice of departures rather than a single option.

What the reply contains

Everything you need to fix a booking

  1. Vessel and voyage

    The named vessel, voyage number and the carrier operating the service.

  2. ETD, ETA and transit

    Departure and arrival dates, the transit time in days, and the transhipment port where there is one.

  3. Cut-offs

    CFS or gate-in cut-off, documentation cut-off and VGM cut-off — the three deadlines that decide whether cargo sails.

  4. Rate and validity

    Where a rate is requested with the schedule, the all-in freight and the date it is valid until.
