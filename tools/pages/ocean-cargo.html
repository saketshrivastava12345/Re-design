The four services

How your cargo crosses the water

LCL

Less Than Container Load

LCL stands for “Less than Container Load”. It is a flexible and cost-effective container option where the shipper shares container space with the shipments that belong to other people.

You pay for the volume you actually occupy, which is why the chargeable weight calculation matters — and why we publish the calculator rather than keep it in a quotation.

Work out your CBM
FCL

Full Container Load

FCL stands for “Full Container Load”. It is a single container booked by a shipper exclusively to transport goods for enhanced safety and security.

The box is sealed at your premises and opened at the consignee's. Fewer handovers, fewer hands, and a shorter chain of custody on high-value cargo.

NVOCC

Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier

Non-Vessel Operating Common Carriers is a revered organization in the global logistics industry. GML issues its own house bill of lading and consolidates as a master.

1650 destinations are serviced by LCL weekly.

CFS

Container Freight Station

Container Freight Operations are performed at CFS [Container Freight Station], a warehouse where consolidation and deconsolidation of Cargo take place.

This is where an LCL shipment is built and broken, where customs examination happens, and where most of a transit's avoidable delay is either created or prevented.

Common questions

Before you book an ocean shipment

Should I ship LCL or FCL?

The crossover depends on the lane, but the arithmetic starts with volume: work out your CBM and chargeable weight in the dimension calculator, then compare it against the capacity of a 20ft box in the container table. Once your cargo fills a meaningful share of a container, FCL usually wins on both cost and handling risk.

How is LCL freight charged?

On the greater of the volume in CBM or the weight in tonnes — the revenue tonne. One CBM is treated as 1,000 kg. Dense cargo is therefore charged on weight and light cargo on volume, which is exactly what the calculator shows you before you commit.

Can you handle hazardous cargo by sea?

Yes, across all nine classes, subject to carrier and port acceptance for the specific UN number. Declare it at enquiry stage with the MSDS — see Hazardous Cargo.

Do you issue your own bill of lading?

Yes. GML is registered as a Multimodal Transport Operator with the Directorate General of Shipping (MTO/DGS/369/MAR/2025) and holds a Federal Maritime Commission bond for United States traffic.
