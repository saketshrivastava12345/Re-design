How air freight is charged

Chargeable weight, and why it is rarely the scale weight

An aircraft runs out of space long before it runs out of lift, so air freight is charged on the greater of the actual gross weight and the volumetric weight.

The volumetric weight uses the IATA divisor of 6,000: length × width × height in centimetres, divided by 6,000, gives kilograms. A carton of 120 × 100 × 100 cm is 200 kg volumetric — whatever the scale says.

Which means the way cargo is packed changes the invoice. Half a centimetre of unnecessary carton on every side of a hundred pieces is real money, and it is worth finding out before the booking rather than after.

Common questions

Before you book an air shipment

What do you need to quote?

Origin and destination airports or door addresses, the number of pieces with dimensions per piece, gross weight, commodity and Incoterm. For dangerous goods, the UN number, class, packing group and the MSDS.

Can you carry dangerous goods by air?

Yes, subject to carrier acceptance for the specific UN number and packing group. Lithium batteries in particular are accepted on a per-carrier, per-state basis — send us the cell specification and the packing instruction number and the desk will confirm before you pack.

Do you handle customs clearance too?

Yes, at both ends. GML holds Authorized Economic Operator status with Indian Customs, which is what keeps clearance and examination interventions to a minimum on a time-critical air shipment.

What is a third country shipment?

Cargo moving between two other countries — say Shanghai to Rotterdam — contracted, documented and paid for through GML India. The group and network offices handle both ends.

Need it on the next flight?

Send the dimensions and the airports. The air desk quotes against confirmed allocation.
