Hazardous Cargo

All nine classes, by sea, surface and air

A Hazmat (also known as dangerous or hazardous material) is any material that poses a serious threat to health, property and safety when transported.

GML is one of the expert international shipping companies in handling hazardous goods. We are competent to cater to diversified business needs including hazardous cargo, with trained teams ensuring compliance across every mode.

Hazardous work is decided long before the truck arrives. Classification, packing group, the right UN-approved packaging, the correct marks and labels, a declaration that matches the MSDS, and a carrier that will actually accept the UN number on that lane. Get those right and the shipment is ordinary. Get one wrong and it stops at the gate.

The nine classes

Classification drives everything downstream — packaging, segregation, documentation, acceptance and cost.

How we handle it

Compliance is a sequence, not a stamp

  1. Classification review

    We start from your MSDS: UN number, proper shipping name, class and subsidiary risk, packing group. If the sheet and the declaration disagree, we sort that out before anything is booked.

  2. Mode and acceptance check

    Not every UN number is accepted on every carrier, every port or every aircraft. The desk confirms acceptance for the specific lane before quoting, so a booking is not withdrawn after your cargo has already moved.

  3. Packing, marking and labelling

    UN-approved packaging to the applicable packing instruction, correct labels and placards, and segregation planning for consolidated loads under the IMDG segregation table.

  4. Declaration and documentation

    The dangerous goods declaration, the container packing certificate, and the mode-specific paperwork — IMDG at sea, IATA DGR by air, ADR-equivalent rules on the road leg.

Send the MSDS with your first enquiry. Almost every avoidable hazardous delay comes from a missing or outdated safety data sheet. With it, the desk can confirm acceptance and give you a real rate in one pass.

Common questions

Hazardous cargo, in practice

Can you consolidate hazardous cargo into LCL?

Depending on class, packing group and what else is in the box. The IMDG segregation table governs what may travel together, and some combinations are simply prohibited. Where consolidation is not permitted, we will say so and price the alternative rather than load it and hope.

What about lithium batteries?

Class 9, and the most heavily restricted cargo in routine commerce. Acceptance depends on the cell chemistry, watt-hour rating, state of charge, whether the cells ship alone, packed with equipment or contained in equipment, and the individual carrier's own embargoes. Send the cell specification and the applicable packing instruction and the desk will confirm before you pack.

Do you handle Class 1 and Class 7?

Explosives and radioactive material are handled case by case. Both need specific carrier acceptance, competent authority approvals and, usually, longer lead times. Bring them to us early.

Which mode is cheapest for hazardous cargo?

Usually sea, sometimes by a wide margin — air acceptance for dangerous goods is restrictive and priced accordingly. But the honest answer depends on the UN number: some classes move easily in a container and barely at all in a hold.

Send the MSDS and the lane.

We will confirm acceptance, packaging and the real cost before you commit.
